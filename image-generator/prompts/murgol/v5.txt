Cinematic wildlife photograph, wide panoramic shot, natural muted colours, subtle filmic grade. Three Múrgol on vast flat red dust plains at sunset. The Múrgol is an enormous, massively heavy herbivore, wider than a bull, with a huge rounded hump and a body like a giant haystack of fur. Its coat is extremely long, dry, coarse and frizzy, hanging in thick matted dreadlocks almost to the ground, mixed ash-grey and charcoal, with long streaks of dark rusty red where red dust has caked into it. Its antlers grow from the sides of the head and reach out horizontally to the left and right, wider than the body, then fork into many crooked tines pointing up and forwards; they look like old gnarled driftwood, grey-brown and rough, knotted and cracked. All three have these antlers. The male stands in the centre of the frame some distance away, facing the camera head-on, his whole body and legs visible with plenty of ground in front of him, shaggy fringe over a broad dark muzzle, black eyes with a faint yellow glint. The other two stand further back, one on each side, three-quarter view with their heads lowered, grazing, their antlers clearly visible. Foreground of dry orange grass tufts on red earth, distant low mountains in haze, dramatic orange sunset clouds filling the sky, low sun just above the horizon on the right, warm golden rim light on the fur.
